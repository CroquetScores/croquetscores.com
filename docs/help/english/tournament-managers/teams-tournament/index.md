# Running a tournament played by teams

A tournament played by teams is one where sides meet, rather than players entered one by one: a test series between two countries, an interstate shield, a world team championship. Its teams are set up with their players, the tests between teams are added, and each test's games go up as they are played. This guide takes a manager from signing in to a tournament whose results are available to the public.

It stands on its own. Nothing in it depends on the guide to a standard tournament, which is written separately.

## Before you start

You need an account. Click [here](/account/register) to register an account.

Everything a manager does is done in the admin section of the website. The public pages, where players and spectators follow the tournament, are drawn from what the admin holds, so nothing has to be published: a score entered is on the public pages at once.

## The words

A **team** is a side, such as Australia, with a name, a short name such as "AUS", and a flag. A team has **players** of its own, who play for it and for nobody else. A **test** is a contest between two teams, called by their short names, as "AUS v ENG", made of a number of **matches** between a player of each team, each match the best of some number of **games**. Tests may be put in **groups**, as "Block A", where many teams play among themselves.

## How the admin is laid out

The admin's front door lists the tournaments you have a part in, one year at a time.

![The list of tournaments in the admin](images/admin-list.png)

Pressing a tournament's name, or **Manage**, opens its page. Everything there is to run is in six tabs beneath its name: **Teams**, **Tests**, **Notes**, **Managers**, **Scorers** and **Rankings**. The page opens on Teams, since the teams are the first thing set up.

![A tournament's page, on its Tests tab](images/tournament-page.png)

The dark green bar beneath the header is for moving between pages. On the tournament's page and on a test's page it leads to the tournament, as **Tournament**, and to each test by its name. On a team's page it leads to the tournament and to each team by its short name instead, since a manager on one team's page is entering players team by team. The page you are on is marked in black.

## Things that hold everywhere

**Nothing is deleted outright except a note.** Teams, players, tests, scores and the tournament itself are archived rather than deleted: set aside, struck through, and listed under a heading of their own, with **Restore** to bring them back. Archiving, restoring and moving up and down never ask you to confirm, because each is undone by pressing the other way. The things that cannot be undone, such as deleting a note or removing a manager, ask first.

**A dialog over the page keeps nothing until you press its button.** Cancel, or the Escape key, closes it and leaves everything as it was. Dialogs that add things, such as Add Team and Add Score, stay open after each one is added, so that a list can be typed in one after another.

**What is refused is said beside the field it is about.** Nothing refused is kept, and what you typed stays typed, so that it can be put right rather than typed again.

**The admin works on a phone.** Every button is big enough to press with a thumb, and nothing is wider than the screen.
