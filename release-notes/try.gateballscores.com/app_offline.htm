Why you may still be seeing this page

Moving a website means changing its address on the internet, which is held in the Domain Name System, or DNS. Think of DNS as the internet's phone book: it tells your device which computer to call when you type try.gateballscores.com.

We have to change that entry, and every internet provider, router and device keeps its own copy of the old entry for a while before it asks for the new one. Until your copy is updated, your device may keep calling the old home, and that is what shows you this page. It is not a fault with your device or your connection, and it will fix itself, usually within a few hours and in rare cases within a day.

If you are seeing this page, try these things

Try them in this order, and check the site again after each one.

  1. Wait a little and reload. Hold down Shift as you press the reload button, or press Ctrl+F5 (Command+Shift+R on a Mac), so the browser asks again and does not show a saved copy of this page.
  2. Close the browser completely and open it again. On a phone or tablet, close the app and open it again, or restart the device. Browsers keep their own copy of the phone book, and restarting clears it.
  3. Try another connection. If you are on Wi-Fi, turn it off and use mobile data, or the other way round. A different network usually has a fresher copy.
  4. Restart your router. Switch it off, wait a minute, and switch it on again. Many routers keep their own copy of the old entry.
  5. Clear your device's saved copy of the phone book.
    • Windows: open the Start menu, type cmd, open Command Prompt, and run ipconfig /flushdns
    • Mac: open Terminal and run sudo dscacheutil -flushcache; sudo killall -HUP mDNSResponder
    • Chrome or Edge, on any computer: go to chrome://net-internals/#dns (or edge://net-internals/#dns) and press “Clear host cache”
    • iPhone, iPad and Android: turn Airplane mode on for ten seconds, then turn it off
  6. Ask a public DNS service instead. If you are comfortable changing network settings, set your device or router to use 1.1.1.1 and 1.0.0.1 (Cloudflare) or 8.8.8.8 and 8.8.4.4 (Google). These services usually have the new entry sooner than your provider does. You can change it back later.
